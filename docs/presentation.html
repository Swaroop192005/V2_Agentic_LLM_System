Final-Year Project

An Agentic Multi-LLM
Answer-Validation System

Can a system of language models decide, on its own, whether an answer is trustworthy — with no human answer key to check against?

Generators LLaMA 3 · Mistral Verifier phi3 Judge qwen3.5:9b Dataset 5,000 questions
01 · The Problem

A model can't grade its own homework

  • 1Ask a language model a hard question and it answers fluently — whether or not it's correct, and it can't reliably tell you which.
  • 2Having a second model "check" the first fails quietly — checkers share the same biases and rubber-stamp plausible text.
  • 3In this domain there is no ground-truth answer key — so accuracy can't be measured directly. Every design choice must be justified another way.
02 · The Idea

Trust through independent corroboration

Instead of one model checking another, use an ensemble that cross-examines itself — and gate the output on how much that ensemble agrees.

Core principle: two models generate, two more evaluate blindly, three external checks corroborate, and one confidence score decides whether to ship the answer or try again.
03 · Architecture

Six stages, one confidence score

STAGE 1–2
Generate
LLaMA 3 + Mistral, independently
STAGE 3
Verify
phi3 scores both, blind
STAGE 5
Judge
qwen3.5:9b scores + picks winner
STAGE 4
Corroborate
Wikipedia · Wikidata · agreement
STAGE 6
Gate + Combine
score ≥ 0.75 or regenerate
The key control: a per-question coin flip randomizes answer order, and evaluators never see which model wrote which — so position and brand can't leak into a score.
04 · Method

Every choice earned its place

With no correctness labels, validity came from two families of label-free test — both of which can fail, which is what makes them real.

Discrimination
Can it tell bad from good?
A usable evaluator must score a deliberately broken answer far below a solid one. This test alone rejected two judge models.
Consistency & validity
Do the signals cohere?
Signals should predict each other, and a low confidence score should genuinely predict that a retry improves the answer.

CompassJudger-1 gave all eight rubric criteria the identical score on 57.5% of answers — a fluent number with no reasoning behind it. Similar averages hid a decisive difference visible only per-criterion.

06 · Finding

Position bias was hiding as quality

Early on, one model was always shown first and prompts named the brands. Once evaluation was made blind and order-randomized, the apparent gap collapsed:

By raw position (artefact)
35% · 65%
A vs B — inflated by the ordering
By true model identity
45% · 55%
LLaMA 3 vs Mistral — the real, small gap

Separating the two proved most of the "gap" was an artefact — and quantified the modest part that was genuine.

07 · Finding

The 0.75 threshold, proven three ways

MethodResult
Two-proportion z-testp = 0.011 — the drop-off is real
ROC / Youden's Joptimal 0.70–0.74, AUC 0.63
2,000-sample bootstrap95% CI [0.69, 0.75]
Solved by design: a control group gave a random slice of already-passing answers a retry too — the only way to get unbiased data above the threshold.
08 · Rigor

Three measurement bugs — caught, not shipped

  • 1A model's self-reported total didn't match its own scores → now always recomputed from the parts.
  • 2A "second-opinion" signal was secretly derived from the judge, inflating results → excluded from the search.
  • 3A threshold analysis under-counted part of the data after a sampling rate changed → re-weighted correctly.

Each was found by a result looking too clean and being checked — and corrected before it became a conclusion.

09 · Honest Limits

What the evidence doesn't settle

The system prefers Mistral (55%). Is that real quality, or a judge quirk?

The Judge says
Mistral is more factual
Higher on correctness, faithfulness, hallucination-freedom.
The Verifier says
No difference at all
Same answers, zero gap on any criterion — and it's proven able to spot real gaps.

Reported as open, with the evidence for both sides — not forced to a tidy conclusion.
